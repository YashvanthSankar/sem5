# Q1 — Scenario 3: The Handloom of Madurai

Time: 45 minutes | Language: C (Linux)

Allowed: fork, wait, exit, pipe, read, write, close, getpid.

Not allowed: threads, semaphores, shared memory, named pipes (FIFOs), perror, rand, struct.

**The Story**
In a handloom workshop in Madurai, the Mill Owner (parent process) is preparing an order of fine cloth. She sends a Weaver (child process)
to the storeroom to count spools of three kinds of thread — Cotton, Silk, Wool. Since the Owner sits in her office and the Weaver works in
the storeroom, they are two separate processes and cannot share a single ledger. The Weaver must pass the spool counts back to the Owner
through a pipe, like shouting the numbers through a narrow window between the rooms. A pipe carries a message only one way, so each side
must decide early on whether it is only listening or only speaking through it.

**The Task**
1. Create a pipe with pipe(fd) before calling fork(), then fork one Weaver process. The Weaver closes the read end of the pipe (he only speaks),
   decides on three counts of his own choosing for Cotton, Silk and Wool spools, and writes each of the three counts to the pipe separately,
   one write() call per thread type. He then closes the write end and exits.
2. The Mill Owner closes the write end of the pipe (she only listens), calls wait() for the Weaver, then read()s the three counts back from
   the pipe one at a time, in the same order they were written, and prints them with their thread names.
3. Explain in 2 lines: what would the Owner's read() return if the Weaver had exited without writing anything, and why must the Owner close
   her own write end before reading?

**Sample Output**
```
Weaver (pid 8351) counted: Cotton=35 Silk=18 Wool=21
```
