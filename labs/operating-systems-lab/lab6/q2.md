# Q2 — Dining Philosophers with a Sauce Pot and "Hungry" Philosophers

[Open the exam solution](q2.c)

Seven philosophers sit around a circular table with seven bowls of noodles but only seven chopsticks
placed between them. Each philosopher needs the two adjacent chopsticks to eat, but now the table also
has a shared pot of sauce that can be used by at most two philosophers at the same time, and every
philosopher must use the sauce once during each meal, while holding both chopsticks. In addition, two of
the philosophers are "hungry" types who must be guaranteed to eat at least once for every two meals taken
by any other philosopher, so no philosopher may be starved indefinitely. Implement a multithreaded C
program using POSIX threads and semaphores in which every philosopher completes 5 meals, ensuring the
program never deadlocks and that no philosopher starves. Print a timestamped log of each philosopher's
actions (thinking, waiting, picking chopsticks, using sauce, eating, releasing) and, at the end, a summary
table showing the number of meals eaten by each philosopher and the maximum waiting time each experienced.

What the question requires, as a checklist:

- 7 philosophers, 7 chopsticks; each needs its left and right chopstick.
- Sauce pot: at most 2 users at once; used once per meal, while holding both chopsticks.
- 2 "hungry" philosophers: each eats at least once for every 2 meals of any other philosopher.
- 5 meals each; no deadlock; no starvation; POSIX threads + semaphores.
- Timestamped log: thinking, waiting, picking chopsticks, using sauce, eating, releasing.
- Summary table: meals per philosopher and maximum waiting time.

## Exam solution

1. Chopstick semaphores start at 1. Philosopher `p` uses `p` and `(p + 1) % 7`. Always pick the lower index first. A circular wait would require the indices to increase around a cycle, which is impossible.
2. Sauce starts at 2. Take sauce after both chopsticks and give it back before eating. Log release before posting so the log reflects the resource order.
3. Philosophers 0 and 1 are hungry. `since[h][p]` counts normal philosopher p's starts since hungry h last started. Under the fairness mutex, normal philosophers wait before taking any chopsticks if either unfinished hungry philosopher's counter is already 2. At an eating start, a hungry philosopher resets its row, and a normal philosopher increments its own column in both rows. A hungry philosopher stops imposing the limit after its fifth start.
4. Time from the waiting message to acquiring both chopsticks. Keep the largest wait and meals eaten per thread. Main reads both arrays after joining all seven threads.

The meal quota applies to **normal philosophers relative to each hungry philosopher** while that hungry philosopher still has meals to start. Lock ordering prevents deadlock. The quota limits normal philosophers' progress relative to hungry ones, and the finite five-meal workload allows everyone to finish under ordinary scheduling.

Thinking, using sauce, and eating use fixed delays. The timestamps and maximum waits still measure what happened in the run.

On Linux, run `gcc -Wall -Wextra q2.c -o q2 -pthread && ./q2`. Expect five meals per person, no adjacent eaters, and no more than two sauce users at once.
